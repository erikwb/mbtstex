# Before submitting

Use this alongside the fourth-edition manual and your assignment instructions.
The class handles formatting; these decisions depend on the actual writing.

- **Text and argument (pp.3–10):** use formal language, clear antecedents,
  consistent pronouns, varied sentences and an identifiable argument. Avoid
  contractions, slang and unnecessary first/second person. Introduce a scholar
  by full name, then use the surname. Proofread aloud and review the PDF.
- **Midwestern conventions (pp.8–9):** capitalize Bible, Scripture, biblical
  divisions/books/proper names, God, and He/Him/His when referring to God.
  Use B.C./A.D. rather than BCE/CE. Spell out one through one hundred in prose,
  including compound-number hyphens, subject to the manual's contextual
  exceptions. Use past tense for historical events; the historical present is
  allowed when analyzing a text, but remain consistent.
- **Sources (pp.31–68):** verify every name, title, publication detail and locator
  against the source. Cite distinctive ideas as well as exact quotations.
  Use the documented BibTeX type and fields, and inspect the finished note.
  Set `shorttitle` for long works. A multi-volume whole-work record needs a
  volume-and-page postnote such as `[2:257]`. Conventional source abbreviations
  and original scripts must accurately identify the work/word.
- **Quotations and mechanics (pp.94–111):** use inline quotes for short passages
  and `mbtsquote` at five or more ordinary-text lines. Put the citation after
  punctuation, without a preceding space. Use single quotation marks for an
  inner quotation. Mark additions with brackets and omissions with spaced
  ellipses, preserving the source's meaning. Paraphrase with genuinely new
  wording and structure, then attribute and cite. Distinguish hyphens (`-`),
  en dashes (`--`) and em dashes (`---`) in TeX. Italicize transliterations, but
  keep Greek/Hebrew in their own upright script and an appropriate font.
- **Scripture (pp.38–39,115–117):** identify the translation in a footnote at the
  first Scripture quotation, giving its name, abbreviation, publication city,
  publisher, and year. Indicate exceptions, use SBL abbreviations in citations and full book names in
  prose. Use commas between verses in the same chapter and semicolons when
  changing chapter/book. Follow the instructor's parenthetical/footnote choice.
  Scripture normally does not go in the bibliography.
- **Bibliography:** ensure every required source is present. For web/blog/social
  content and podcasts, opt in to the bibliography only when important or
  frequently cited. Live performances and denominational resolutions/reports
  stay in notes. For several manuscript items, cite the collection as a whole
  in the bibliography and mark item records `keywords={notes-only}`. Sort manual
  entries by author and then title. Avoid splitting an entry unnecessarily.
- **Book reviews (pp.71–75):** include the opening bibliography entry with page
  count and price; an author sketch of about half a page; a contents summary of
  about two pages; and critical evaluation of about two to five pages. Cite
  the reviewed book parenthetically even when other sources appear in notes.
  More than two outside sources requires a bibliography of those sources.
- **Annotated bibliographies (p.83):** alphabetize entries and meet the
  instructor's requirements for annotation length and content.
- **Dissertations (pp.84–93):** obtain the applicable program guide. Include the
  supplied approval, title, copyright, contents, abstract, bibliography and vita
  in the required order; keep optional pages in the prescribed positions. Verify
  the abstract limit and chapter plan. DMin/DEdMin conclusions belong at the end
  of chapter six; PhD/counseling works conclude in their final chapter. A fifth
  heading level is PhD-only and requires chair approval; second-level contents
  entries also require permission. The vita is the final page.
- **Visuals (pp.112–114):** keep objects at or below half a page near their
  discussion; put larger ones in appendices and cross-reference them. Check
  uppercase labels/titles, headline column headings, a stub first column,
  centered numbers, the table's double top rule, source/explanation lines, and
  grayscale legibility. Ordinary LaTeX floats need manual placement review.
- **Final PDF:** switch to `submission`, confirm Times New Roman is embedded,
  resolve all build warnings, remove placeholders, and inspect page breaks,
  orphaned headings, margins, missing glyphs and numbering. A successful compile
  alone does not check the factual or academic requirements above.
